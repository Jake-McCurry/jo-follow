Foundation

Design strategy

The palette pairs a confident, trustworthy blue architecture with a restrained warm signal for invitation and human connection.

The system is deliberately asymmetric: blue carries the brand; orange creates emphasis. This hierarchy prevents visual competition and gives every screen a calm, recognizable rhythm.

Trust before excitement

Deep and mid blues establish credibility. Bright blue is a signal, not a surface default.

Warmth with restraint

Orange marks invitations, moments of hope, and selected actions. It should feel meaningful because it is used sparingly.

Depth through value

Build hierarchy with lightness—from pale atmospheric backgrounds to navy structure—rather than adding unrelated hues.

Accessible by design

Text pairings are assigned by measured contrast. Decorative colors never carry essential meaning alone.

Recommended master proportion: 65 / 25 / 10
65% neutral space25% blue10% warm

For editorial and reading experiences, increase neutral space to 75–85%. For campaign moments, blue may rise to 35%, but orange should rarely exceed 10% of the visible composition.

Brand character

Clear · hopeful · grounded · welcoming · enduring

Visual tension

Cool structure + warm humanity. Saturated signal + generous quiet space.

Critical distinction: accent versus action

Warm 500 is the recognizable orange but reaches only 2.96:1 against white. Use it decoratively or with Navy text. For white text on an orange button, use Warm 700, which reaches 4.64:1 and passes WCAG AA.

Invitational actions

Color relationship rules

  • Place orange near blue to create focus, not across every blue surface.
  • Prefer warm paper over cool gray when a softer, pastoral tone is desired.
  • Use Slate for supporting copy; reserve Navy for essential reading and hierarchy.
  • Use Blue 500 at small-to-medium scale. For large fields, move to Blue 800 or Navy.

Interaction

Maintain a 3:1 boundary contrast for controls and focus indicators. Never use color as the sole signal for errors, selection, or completion.

Typography

Body text: minimum 16px. Captions: minimum 12px. Playfair Display is reserved for large hero and page titles; Source Sans 3 handles all interface copy.

Accessibility operating rule

When in doubt, move darker—not more saturated. Darker blue preserves the brand while improving legibility; darker orange preserves warmth while supporting white text.

Contrast standards06
Application

Usage architecture

A consistent role assignment is more valuable than using every shade. These rules make the palette scalable across teams and years.

Hero surfaces

Default to Blue 800. Add subtle Blue 700 or Blue 900 depth. Never use Blue 500 as a full hero field.

Navigation

Blue 500 is the recognizable menu signal. Navy may be used for premium or content-dense navigation.

Reading surfaces

Warm Paper or White with Navy text. Blue 50 and Warm 50 support low-intensity section changes.

Primary actions

Use Blue 800 with white for dependable actions. Use Warm 700 for a single invitational action per view.

Decorative emphasis

Warm 500 marks rules, underlines, icons, pull quotes, and moments of hope. Keep areas small.

Borders and states

Blue 100 for soft cards, Border Blue for controls, Blue 500 for focus, Blue 50 for selected backgrounds.

Do

  • Create hierarchy through lightness.
  • Allow large neutral areas.
  • Use one warm focal point.
  • Test final color pairs in context.

Avoid

  • Equal areas of bright blue and orange.
  • Blue 500 as a page-sized background.
  • White body copy on Warm 500.
  • Unapproved teal, purple, or yellow accents.
Long-term governance

New colors should solve a documented functional need, pass contrast review, and fit the existing blue/warm hue families. Do not add colors merely to create novelty.
