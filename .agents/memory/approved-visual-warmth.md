---
name: Approved visual warmth
description: The user-approved visual direction for Follow Jesus Online.
---

Use the branding guide’s “cool structure + warm humanity” direction consistently across the project: the global navigation uses bright brand blue #0095FF, while deep navy remains the structural and text color. Primary and secondary copy on light surfaces use #003A66 deep navy. Orange is a restrained invitation/accent rather than a dominant field color. Actions should use bold, larger type and accessible darker blue or orange backgrounds with white text.

**Why:** The user explicitly confirmed that the warm-paper cards and stronger, higher-contrast actions were the right direction and asked for that treatment across the entire site.

**How to apply:** Carry this direction into new and revised public pages, reading surfaces, supporting screens, and shared components. Preserve generous editorial space and avoid turning every surface orange.

The approved homepage hero keeps the reference image framing, with the headline and subtitle vertically centered over it. At narrower desktop widths, scale the hero height to retain that framing rather than retaining a fixed desktop height that exposes extra sky.

**Why:** The user repeatedly clarified that they wanted the live hero image unchanged, with the text moved down, then explicitly approved the matching-width preview. Their narrower Replit preview still showed extra sky after cache clearing because the fixed-height responsive crop differed from the approved wider reference.

**How to apply:** Compare the actual narrower Replit preview as well as the reference width, not just matching-width screenshots. Preserve the approved framing across desktop widths and keep text placement independent of the image.

For the Adventure Guide series, use a warmer chapter-like reading experience with subtle cream-background breadcrumbs, editorial display headings, warm quote/question callouts, and an inviting next-chapter moment. Keep this treatment isolated from Go Deeper articles.

**Why:** The user found the standard article treatment too cold for the Adventure series and approved a more engaging guided-journey direction inspired by a warm editorial chapter reference.

**How to apply:** Preserve the distinct Adventure treatment across all current and future Adventure chapters without copying the reference literally. A later user request explicitly extended that same book framing to Go Further, superseding the earlier Adventure-only restriction; it does not extend to Go Deeper. Keep opening paragraphs in the article body, sharing visually quiet, reactions to one compact thumbs-up/down row, and the bottom choices compact but visibly separate. “Read the next chapter” is the larger dark-blue primary card; Go Deeper remains a distinct optional secondary card.

The Explore Articles page should remain icon-free; its cards and section links rely on typography, spacing, borders, and restrained color rather than decorative symbols or directional arrows.

**Why:** Repeated icons consumed too much card space and made the article catalog feel busier than its content required.

**How to apply:** Do not reintroduce per-card icons, section-header icons, or arrow icons on Explore Articles. Keep cards compact and text-led.

Keep “Connect with God” compact on mobile. Use the supplied three-card grouping as the reference rather than nine spacious standalone cards.

**Why:** The user said the nine cards “need to take up significantly less space, so it’s less scrolling on mobile,” explaining that the document grouped the resources “inside 3 total cards.” They approved the compact three-card result.

**How to apply:** Retain the nine resource options but reduce card padding and repeated action rows. Do not expand this section's mobile footprint during future visual changes.

Keep the homepage hero compact and show the supplied photograph without a gradient overlay.

**Why:** The user first requested a lighter gradient and shorter hero, then requested removing the gradient entirely. They also requested a bolder subtitle, the title above the group's heads, and a crop at the center figure's ankles.

**How to apply:** Preserve the shorter, image-led treatment, keeping the main title above the group's heads and the lower crop at the center figure's ankles. Maintain text contrast with bold white type and a local text shadow, not an image overlay. Do not restore the gradient during later hero changes.

Use individual title-and-paragraph cards with plain, unnumbered navy links and darker orange triple chevrons on the Prayer Starter Guide.

**Why:** The user clarified that removing list bullets also meant removing numeric prefixes embedded in titles. They requested subtle warmth after finding the first tint too strong, darker hover treatment for links above the content, and a separate card for each article title and its paragraph instead of one shared card. They later replaced title arrows with darker orange double chevrons and asked that they follow the final word when a title wraps.

**How to apply:** Treat “just the titles” on this page as excluding both list markers and numbers embedded in imported title text. Keep each article's description paired with its title and preserve link destinations. Individual article cards and darker topic-navigation hovers remain specific to `/prayer`. The user extended the chevron replacement to existing arrows across the website; do not add new icons to otherwise icon-free Explore Articles cards.

Use moderately sized, close-spaced triple chevrons for the shared directional navigation style. Right-pointing chevrons go after the final word; left-pointing chevrons go before the text.

**Why:** After requesting the site-wide replacement, the user found the larger double chevrons too big, requested a smaller compromise with three closer chevrons, and clarified that their side must match their pointing direction.

**How to apply:** Keep right-pointing chevrons attached to the final word when wrapping; keep left-pointing chevrons attached to the first word. Preserve navigation destinations and readable contrast on dark buttons. This replaces existing arrow affordances, not disclosure indicators or previously icon-free links.

Article sharing is a quiet, icon-only utility at the far right of the breadcrumb row, not a call to action beneath the heading. Breadcrumbs should be smaller.

**Why:** The user wants sharing available for readers who seek it out, without prominent “Share this article” text. On Prayer articles it belongs to the right of the article progress indicator.

**How to apply:** Apply this consistently across article and book-reading layouts. Keep any progress indicator before the share icon, preserve sharing metadata and functionality, and retain an accessible name and usable touch target even though the visible icon is small.

Use the `/prayer` page as the template for The Lord’s Prayer Guide.

**Why:** The user explicitly asked to use the Prayer Starter page as the template when building The Lord’s Prayer Guide.

**How to apply:** Keep the same individual warm topic-card pattern, navy link titles, shared chevrons, and native reading navigation rather than introducing a separate visual style.

Use the approved subtle cream cards against a lighter page background across the homepage and other public card-based sections.

**Why:** After the Prayer page test, the user explicitly requested applying its card and page colors to the homepage and other places using that contrast.

**How to apply:** Keep neutral cards and page surfaces consistent without changing navigation/sidebar palettes, primary action colors, content, or card groupings. This approval extends the colors, not the Prayer-specific link and layout treatments.

Lord’s Prayer Guide illustrated cards should blend into their surrounding warm background without visible white backing.

**Why:** The user requested removing the white backing and confirmed the result with “good work.”

**How to apply:** Preserve the colored artwork and text when displaying or replacing these illustrations; avoid reintroducing white frames or gaps around the colored panels.
