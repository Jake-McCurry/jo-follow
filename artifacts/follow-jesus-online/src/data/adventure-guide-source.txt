Begin the Adventure
Something real has begun in your life.

When you asked Jesus Christ to come in, you stepped into a personal relationship with God that will never end.
The One who created you now lives within you, and Jesus promises to take you as you are and make your life
extraordinary, Christ-centered, and purposeful. It is the beginning of a new way of living—with Jesus Himself as
your leader.

My Heart, Christ’s Home
Many years ago, Robert Boyd Munger described what it felt like when he first invited Jesus into his life. He
pictured his heart as a home, and Jesus as the welcomed guest who began to settle in:

“One evening I invited Jesus Christ into my heart. What an entrance He made! It was not a spectacular,
emotional thing, but very real. Something happened at the very center of my life. He came into the darkness of
my heart and turned on the light. He built a fire on the hearth and banished the chill. He started music where
there had been stillness, and He filled the emptiness with His own loving, wonderful fellowship.”

Munger then imagined Jesus walking through each “room”—his thoughts, his desires, his relationships, his work,
his free time, even the private places he felt ashamed to open to anyone. In every room, Jesus offered to bring
light, order, and life.

Munger’s story illustrates how Jesus will transform your life from the inside out as you surrender every area to
Him and let Him be in charge. This booklet shows you how to begin that adventure: to become all God created
you to be, so that you can do all He created you to do.

Walking the Path Ahead
The Christian life is more like a marathon than a sprint. There will be days of clarity and days of confusion,
moments of triumph and moments of defeat, ordinary days and unexpected events. What remains constant is
the presence of Christ and the patient work of His Spirit within you. He has already given you everything you
need to take this journey.

What You Will Discover
This booklet is a guide for your new life in Christ. In the lessons ahead, you will explore:

 • Citizen of Heaven — the assurance of your eternal belonging and secure future with God
 • Your New Identity in Christ — who you truly are now that you belong to Him
 • The Holy Spirit — Your Constant Companion — the power and presence of God living in you
 • Walking by Faith, Not by Feelings — how to trust God when circumstances press hard
 • God’s Word — Your Road Map — the Bible as a reliable path for your steps
 • Prayer — Your Ongoing Conversation with God — learning to talk with your heavenly Father
 • Belonging to God’s Family — the strength and joy of life with other believers
 • Living a Life of Purpose — how God sees your new life from His eternal purpose

1. Citizen of Heaven
Are you certain you would go to heaven if you died today?

The Bible tells us we can know for sure. Even after inviting Jesus into your life, you may still wonder. This lesson
is written to give you clear, biblical confidence that you belong to Christ—now and always.

God’s Free Gift
Jesus Christ paid the full price for all your sins on the cross. Through His death and resurrection, you have been
given direct access to God. God’s perfect justice has been completely satisfied by the blood of Jesus. He has
purchased you, and He will not take back what He has purchased (Hebrews 7:25; 9:12).

they somehow contribute?” The Bible gives you a clear answer. Read the following verses and write what each

Your Faithful Friend
The Bible tells you that Jesus is the friend who remains closer than any other. He is loyal, present, and
unchanging.

  Ashley’s Story
  “I always put my friends above God. In fact, my life revolved around my friends so much that I began to
  get involved in reckless behavior just to seem ‘cool’ to others. Pretty soon I got involved in drugs, sex and
  alcohol. My life got out of control, and I felt worthless, with no purpose other than to get ‘high.’ Then I
  even lost my friends, who called me ‘trash.’ One day someone told me that Jesus Christ not only created
  me, but wanted to be my best friend. I couldn’t believe it at first. Later, I decided to invite Him into my life.
  Since then, God has given me a new purpose in life and helped me break my addictions. I now have many
  new friends, but the greatest of all is Jesus Christ. He accepts me for who I am, and tells me that He will
  never leave or forsake me.”



Ashley discovered what believers in every nation have discovered: Jesus is a faithful friend. No other friend loves
you so much or will stay with you so closely (Proverbs 18:24). Jesus is totally loyal and faithful (Proverbs 20:6).

Growth Is God’s Plan for You
God does not leave you to figure out the Christian life on your own. From the beginning He has a clear path for
your growth: walk in the Holy Spirit; trust God with your life; know and trust His Word; talk with Him in prayer;
connect with other believers; live a life of purpose.

These are not requirements to earn His love. They are the natural ways a new life in Christ begins to take shape.
He is already committed to the work He has begun.

2. Your New Identity in Christ
The moment you trusted Christ, your identity changed forever. You are no longer defined by your past, your
performance, or others’ opinions. You are now a child of God, with a new nature and a secure place in His
family. You are who God says you are.

C. S. Lewis pointed out: “Mere morality is not the end of life. You were made for something quite different from
that… The idea of reaching ‘a good life’ without Christ is based on a double error. Firstly, we cannot do it; and
secondly… we have missed the very point of our existence.”

The point of our existence centers on the inheritance we have in Christ as His children. Living for Christ is not our
duty as slaves, but our privilege as sons and daughters.

Your Inheritance in Christ
As a Christian, you have been given a remarkable inheritance. Read the following verses and thank God for what
is now true of you:

 • Your sins are completely forgiven (Colossians 2:13–14)
 • You become a child of God—forever (John 1:12)
 • Christ lives in you (Galatians 2:20; Colossians 1:27)
 • The Holy Spirit is your guarantee (2 Corinthians 1:22)
 • Christ gives you His new nature (2 Corinthians 5:17)

A New You
   Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here! (2 Corinthians
   5:17)

You are now a completely new creation—like a butterfly that was once a caterpillar. You may not always feel
new or act new, but in God’s eyes you are fully new. The Father sees you as the one His Son purchased with His
own blood. You are His royal child (Hebrews 2:9–11).

   You also, like living stones, are being built into a spiritual house… You are a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a
   holy nation, God’s special possession. (1 Peter 2:5, 9)

It does not matter what your background, abilities, appearance, or status may be. You now belong to Christ’s
body with believers from every time and place. You are a full citizen of heaven (Ephesians 2:19).

3. The Holy Spirit — Your Constant Companion

Ready in Heart, but Not Yet Empowered
The disciples were deeply moved after the resurrection and eager to follow Jesus wherever He led. Yet even
after three years of walking with Him, they still lacked the power to live the life He had called them to live.
Enthusiasm alone would not be enough.

Jesus knew they needed more. He told them, “You will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you” (Acts
1:8). Before leaving, He instructed them to wait for the promise of the Father: “I am going to send you what my
Father has promised” (Luke 24:49).

The Holy Spirit is given many names in the Bible. Among the roles He plays in your life, He is your Encourager
and Comforter as you walk with Christ (John 16:7–15). Without Him, living for Christ is impossible. With Him,
you can do all things through the strength He supplies (Philippians 4:13).

The Indwelling Holy Spirit
When you invited Jesus Christ to be your Savior and Lord, He entered your life through the Holy Spirit, and He
will remain with you forever. You are never alone.

Ephesians 3:16–17 says that God strengthens you with power through His Spirit in your inner being, so that
Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith. As Robert Munger pictured it, Jesus desires to settle into every
room of your life. Christ lives in you by His Spirit.

The Battle Within
Every day you face influences from within and from the world that pull you away from God and go against His
will—temptations, anger, addictions, jealousy, and other destructive patterns (Galatians 5:19–21). Even good
things such as family, success, and security can come between God and you when they become the center of
your life. True satisfaction comes only when you give Jesus His rightful place: the throne of your life (Matthew
6:33).

In Romans 7, Paul writes about his desire to live for Christ and his repeated failures. This battle of the flesh
versus the Spirit can only be won as we surrender our wills to the Holy Spirit. Romans 8 shows the victory that
follows.

As long as we live in this body, the conflict between self-centered desires and Christ’s way will remain. The
question is which voice we will follow.

A Simple Step of Surrender
Giving control of your life to the Holy Spirit is a decision of faith, just as receiving Christ was. You place yourself
in the hands of a trustworthy God.

You might pray: “Lord Jesus, I want my life to be directed by You rather than by self. I confess the ways I have
lived independently of You. Holy Spirit, I ask You to take control of my life right now.”

Restoring Fellowship
At times believers sin and break fellowship with the Holy Spirit (1 John 1:8). This does not mean your salvation is
lost. Jesus paid for all your sins on the cross, once for all. Broken fellowship means you are not abiding in Christ.
You are back on the throne of your life, ignoring Him. If it is not mended, you will not experience the abundant
life Jesus promised and will begin to miss His purpose and plan for your life.

                                                 JesusOnline Ministries   ·   Page 11
You can return at any moment: yield yourself again to God; confess your specific sins; trust that the Spirit is in
control.




Spiritual Breathing
Walking in the Spirit is a moment-by-moment dependence on Him. Bill Bright called this spiritual breathing.
Exhale—confess the sin that grieves Him. Inhale—ask the Spirit by faith to fill and direct you again. He is always
ready to restore and empower you.




Bearing Fruit
The Holy Spirit will empower you not only to defeat the flesh but also to bear fruit. Jesus compared your life to a
branch that is totally dependent on the grapevine. The gardener cares for the branches, expecting to harvest
grapes. They draw nutrients from the vine to produce fruit.

   I am the vine; you are the branches. If you remain in me and I in you, you will bear much fruit; apart from me you
   can do nothing. (John 15:5)

One kind of fruit your life in Christ produces is the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness,
goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. You do not manufacture it. You abide, and it grows
naturally. This fruit shapes your life so that God can use it to reach others for Christ. (Read John chapter 15)

own strength, but letting Him live His life through us by faith—trusting Jesus enough to do whatever He tells us

Overcoming Doubt

  Joni’s Story
  Joni Eareckson was an active, athletic teenager until the day she dove into shallow water and broke her
  neck. In a moment the future she had imagined was gone. She would never walk or use her hands again.
  Although she had trusted Christ as a teenager, the months that followed shook her deeply. “In those first
  few months,” she later wrote, “the promises of God seemed anything but true… How could I be expected
  to believe when everything inside and outside of me screamed just the opposite?” Through the steady
  encouragement of a friend and the truth of God’s Word, Joni began to choose faith over her feelings.
  Looking back, she has said that her paralysis was not a cruel joke but was used by God’s love. Today she
  leads an international ministry that shares the hope of Christ with people affected by disability. Her life
  reminds us that victorious faith is not the absence of struggle. It is trusting God even when we cannot yet
  see the reason.

5. God’s Word — Your Road Map
If you received a personal letter from Jesus telling you how much He loves you and offering clear direction for
your daily life, would you read it carefully? Would you return to it often?

That is what the Bible is—God’s own word to you. Just as a new baby needs milk to grow, you need God’s Word
to grow in your new life.

Before You Begin
If you do not have a printed Bible, open the NET Bible at follow.jesusonline.com/bible/ and type the verse listed
in the lesson. The King James Version (KJV) is common, but the English language is old. A modern translation like
NET Bible is easier to understand. Free online Bibles in many languages are available at bible.com.

God’s Road Map for Your Life
If you were traveling to a place you had never been, would you rely only on instinct, or would you use a reliable
map? The map will not show every detail along the road, but it will keep you from heading the wrong way. The
Bible is the road map for your journey with God. It guides you with the truth you need for life.

Forming a New Habit
Most of what we do each day is out of habit. To make God’s Word part of your life, you need to make it one of
your daily habits. Choose a consistent time and a quiet place. Read a section of the Bible and ask God to show
you what He wants you to understand and apply. Be patient and persistent. It takes time to develop a new habit.
The more you let His Word dwell in you richly (Colossians 3:16), the more you will experience His presence and
power.

God’s Viewpoint
The Bible gives God’s answers to the most important questions of life: who God is; your purpose; how to grow in
Christ; moral decisions; relationships; peace in hardship; suffering and death.

   For the word of God is alive and active. Sharper than any double-edged sword, it penetrates even to dividing soul
   and spirit… it judges the thoughts and attitudes of the heart. (Hebrews 4:12)

What Fills Your Mind
Every day your mind is flooded with voices—most of them reflecting the world’s perspective. What you allow to
fill your “vat” will influence your decisions, attitudes, and direction. If God’s perspective is to shape your life, you
must give regular time and serious effort to keep filling the vat with His Word.

In today’s world driven by social media, filling your “vat” with God’s truth is ever more critical for you to live a
Christ-directed, Spirit-empowered life.

6. Prayer — Your Ongoing Conversation with God
Imagine you are having lunch with a friend when your phone rings. An unfamiliar voice speaks your name and
says, “This is your Heavenly Father. I have been wanting to talk with you.” How would you respond—surprised,
hesitant, grateful, or unsure?

God desires that kind of open conversation with you. He wants you to know Him well enough that you feel free
to come to Him at any time, even when you have sinned. The father who runs toward his returning son in Luke
15:11–32 reveals God’s heart toward you.

Prayer is one of the great privileges of belonging to Christ. Through Jesus, you have direct access to your
Heavenly Father. He invites you to bring Him whatever is on your heart, and He promises to listen. Three truths
support a growing life of prayer:

 • The Holy Spirit helps you pray
 • Faith makes prayer effective
 • God’s Word teaches you who He is and how to pray

Relationship, Not Rules
Every healthy relationship depends on honest communication. Your Father wants you to seek His guidance,
peace, and companionship at all times, and not only when you need something, because He delights in you
(Galatians 4:6). Jesus said, “Ask and you will receive, and your joy will be complete” (John 16:24).

Prayer deepens and strengthens your relationship with God. He not only delights in you but also gives you to
make your joy complete. Prayer is all about interacting with and knowing God.

Knowing Your Father
The more clearly you see who God is, the more you will want to talk with Him. His glorious being is far beyond
full human understanding, yet the Bible gives a glimpse of His character: sovereign, eternal, all-knowing, present
everywhere, all-powerful, unchanging, holy, just, loving, and completely trustworthy.

As you consider each of these, see how it affects your view of God and your relationship with Him.

Praying with Confidence and Humility
   Let us then approach God’s throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help
   us in our time of need. (Hebrews 4:16)

You are always welcome in His presence because you are His beloved child. At the same time, His sovereignty
and holiness demand that you approach Him with humility. Pride resists dependence. Humility opens the way
for His help (1 Peter 5:5–6). Your confidence in prayer rests on the following truths:

You belong to Him. God hears the prayers of His children. He also hears those who turn to Christ for the first
time.

wish, and it will be done for you” (John 15:7). A life close to Christ becomes a life of prayer according to God’s
will.

Growing in the Practice of Prayer
Beginning the day: A simple way to strengthen your relationship with Christ is to turn to Him at the start of each
day. Before the demands of the morning take over, you might say: “Father, I love You. I want to walk with You
today. Lead me in what lies ahead.”

Claiming His promises: Carry a verse with you and turn to it throughout the day. Proverbs 3:5–6 is a steady place
to begin.

Restoring fellowship: When you become aware of sin, name it, and choose to let Him control your life. He will
then restore you to fellowship with Him. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins
and purify us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9). The Spirit helps us in our weakness (Romans 8:26–27).

Thanksgiving and adoration. God has already done for you more than you can measure. Many psalms sing
praise and express gratitude to God. Praying through them is a great way to worship Him. Begin with Psalms 95,
107, and 145.

Bringing requests. Your Father wants you to ask. He delights in your requests since it is an act of faith and
obedience. Never quit asking Him for things that are biblical and within His will. He promises to answer those
prayers … in His time!

7. Belonging to God’s Family
When you trusted Christ, you were not only given a new identity. You were placed into a new community. God is
our Heavenly Father; this community, the church, is His family. The Bible also calls the church the body of Christ.
Jesus is the head. We are members of His body.

Christ’s Local Body
All who follow Jesus across every generation and every place make up the Church. The Bible also uses the word
church for a local gathering of believers. Jesus promises, “For where two or three gather in my name, there am I
with them” (Matthew 18:20).

The Example of the Early Church
The book of Acts shows how the first believers lived this family life. They met, shared their lives, and supported
one another with deep commitment. The New Testament letters were written to these early communities, and
the same Spirit who guided them continues to guide the church today.

Their love for one another and steady witness were so compelling that the message of Christ spread through the
Roman world. God worked through ordinary people who took their life together seriously.

Why Connection Matters
One of the most common reasons believers grow weak is isolation. Paul taught that every Christian is a
necessary part of the body. Just as every part of the human body has a role—some visible, some hidden—so

When believers who are growing in Christ share life together, their faith is strengthened, their burdens are
shared, their love for God and others deepens, and they learn from each other. A single coal grows cold when
separated from the fire. Together, coals keep one another burning.

A new identity, walking with the Holy Spirit, faith, the Bible, prayer, and belonging to God’s family are not ends

created us to do.

Personal Holiness
Being holy is one of the primary purposes of becoming all that God created us to be. Holiness is one of God’s
defining attributes. He is morally pure, righteous, and set apart from all. The Holy Spirit has set us apart for God
and works within us to form the holiness of Christ to be holy.

Nancy DeMoss writes: “True holiness starts on the inside–with our thoughts, attitudes, values, and motives–
those innermost parts of our hearts that only God can see. It also affects our outward and visible behavior: ‘Be
holy in all your conduct.’”
